## The problem

After booking, customers come back to the account area to check their trip and make changes. It ran on Sitecore with ASP.NET MVC and Razor views. Even a change that only touched the UI went through two repositories, an extra integration step in Sitecore, and the weekly release.

## How we moved

We replaced the account area step by step, so customers never saw one big switch.

- **Page by page.** Simplest pages first, so we learned the new setup before the complex ones.
- **A bug bash first.** Before booking details went live, the team tested it together. It found no showstoppers, and everything it found was fixed within a week.
- **Market by market.** I ran the rollouts behind feature flags, starting with Belgium and ending with Eliza.
- **A way back.** During the rollout, a banner let customers open the old version. It kept them covered if the new pages had a bug, and showed how the new design landed: fewer than 3 in 100 visits went back.
- **Eyes on every release.** A New Relic dashboard tracked errors and Core Web Vitals for each market as it went live. It caught 500s from Sitecore endpoints that couldn't resolve the site, and a big layout shift on the new booking details page. We fixed both.
- **A clean finish.** Once every market was live, we removed the flags and the legacy routes.

## Booking details

### Editing travellers without leaving the booking

Editing a traveller used to be a separate page. I moved it into a sheet on booking details: one page fewer and several steps shorter. Fields that can’t be saved online become a request to customer service, sent from the same form.

### Flight information

This is where I spent most of my time, because one booking can mix airlines, booking references and stopovers.

- When travellers in one booking fly on different flights, each gets their own section.
- Each direction says whether it’s direct or how many stopovers it has, with the waiting time between flights.
- Each flight shows its booking reference, luggage, and the airline’s links for seats and check-in.
- On a phone, outbound and return sit in tabs, one at a time.

## Results

- Live on 10 sites, 2 brands and 7 countries, with no big-bang launch.
- UI changes ship on demand, usually two or three times a week, instead of in a fixed weekly release.
- Editing a traveller no longer needs a separate page, and 96% of edits saved from the website go through.
- The page no longer jumps while it loads: layout shift (CLS, 75th percentile) went from 0.12 on the old page to almost zero.

## What I learned

- **Small markets first.** Problems stayed small and were fixed before the next market.
- **Design for messy data.** Most of the flight work was the edge cases.
